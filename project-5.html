Vortex Generators:
CFD Analysis

A computational extension of my high-school NACA 2412 wind-tunnel research, using ANSYS Fluent to compare flow separation and investigate experimental limitations.

↗ Original Flow Control Research Project

Project Overview

Independent CFD investigation
NACA 2412 · Low-Reynolds-number flow · ANSYS Fluent 2025 R2

Research Objective

Do the CFD simulations and wind-tunnel experiments predict similar flow separation behaviour for the baseline and vortex-generator (VG) configurations?

Methodology

  • Reconstructed the airfoil geometry in Fusion 360 and replicated the original wind-tunnel domain.
  • Performed steady laminar simulations in Fluent with local mesh refinement around the VGs and near-wall inflation layers.
  • Assessed mesh sensitivity using wall shear stress and aerodynamic coefficients, and visualized the flow using pathlines.
Mesh Sensitivity

0.89% change in lift coefficient, 1.37% in drag coefficient, and a 1.3 mm shift in separation between the two VG meshes.

Mesh sensitivity was assessed using two meshes for one VG configuration. The experimental comparison remains preliminary.

01 · Numerical Methodology

Geometry, Boundary Conditions and Solver Setup

In the original wind-tunnel experiment, smoke visualization was used to identify the angle of attack at which flow separation reached 30% chord. Each selected configuration was simulated at its corresponding experimental criterion angle. The comparison therefore concerns separation at these operating points rather than aerodynamic performance at a common angle of attack.

ParameterSetting

The geometry replicates the original 3D-printed wind-tunnel model. The relatively large VG dimensions were dictated by the resolution limitations of the original printer and were retained for consistency with the experiment. Steady laminar modelling was selected as the baseline given the low chord Reynolds number and the use of a flow-straightening filter at the tunnel inlet. The confined domain and boundary conditions were chosen to reproduce the experimental setup. An additional far-field control simulation was performed to assess tunnel-confinement effects. The far-field domain used symmetry boundaries, with an upstream extent of 275 mm (approximately 3.67c), a downstream extent of 12c = 900 mm, and an extent of 5c on all remaining sides, where c is the chord length.

Separation Results

The CFD simulations predicted separation at approximately 44–49% chord for the control and VG cases, compared with 30% chord in the original experiment. The similar predicted separation locations are qualitatively consistent with the experimental comparison, although CFD predicted separation roughly 14–19 percentage points farther downstream.

The consistent downstream offset suggests a possible systematic discrepancy. Potential sources include uncertainty in the experimental airspeed, geometry and angle of attack, as well as the assumed inlet velocity profile, tunnel-wall mesh resolution, visual separation criterion and steady-flow approximation. The available results do not establish airspeed uncertainty as the cause; additional sensitivity studies are required. Differences between the confined and far-field control cases also indicate sensitivity to domain confinement, a limitation of the original wind-tunnel experiment.

05 · Conclusions and Limitations

Numerical Assessment

  • Evaluated mesh validity and cell quality separately from solution sensitivity to refinement.
  • Assessed iterative convergence using residuals, force histories and mass balance.
  • Used a repeatable wall shear stress diagnostic in place of the visual separation estimate.
  • Identified numerical and experimental sources of uncertainty in the separation comparison.

Limitations and Future Work

  • Improve tunnel-wall boundary-layer resolution and assess sensitivity to inlet velocity.
  • Investigate the oscillatory 11.5° case using transient laminar simulations. The unconverged steady-state simulation was excluded from the reported results.

Methodology references: NASA grid-convergence guidance · NASA validation assessment. All reported simulation data and figures were obtained from my Fluent simulations.
