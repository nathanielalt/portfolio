Tabletop Wind Tunnel

Design, fabrication, and experimental testing of a compact FDM-printed wind tunnel with a custom smoke-delivery system.

Project Overview

Independent Design and Fabrication
Fusion 360 · FDM 3D Printing · Experimental Flow Visualization

Project Objective

Design and fabricate a compact tabletop wind tunnel capable of producing visible smoke streaks within the test section. The tunnel structure, airflow geometry, and smoke-delivery system were designed for ease of fabrication and subsequent modification.

Design and Development

  • Modelled a modular inlet, honeycomb, test section, and downstream diffuser in Fusion 360.
  • Fabricated PLA components using a Bambu Lab A1 mini, iterating on wall thickness and printable geometry.
  • Developed a smoke chamber, injection rake, and return tubing that diverts part of the fan exhaust into the smoke supply.
  • Resolved chamber leakage and heat distortion by incorporating a food-container screw-on cap, switching to incense sticks, and increasing the chamber roof height.
Experimental Results

Visible smoke streaks were obtained during prototype testing. Modifications to the smoke-delivery system improved tracer transport and resolved the chamber-sealing and heat-distortion problems observed during initial testing. Further development focuses on smoke consistency and experimental repeatability.

01 · Aerodynamic and Mechanical Design

Wind Tunnel Configuration

A downstream fan draws air through the inlet and honeycomb into the test section. The inlet contracts toward the test section, while the honeycomb is intended to reduce transverse velocity components. The downstream diffuser gradually expands the flow passage toward the fan.

The test-section height is approximately 80 mm. The overall dimensions were selected to accommodate viewing access, printable component sizes, and the space required for the inlet and diffuser.

Smoke Generation and Injection

A portion of the fan exhaust is redirected into the incense chamber to assist smoke transport toward the injection system. The main tunnel remains open to the surrounding environment, forming an open-circuit wind tunnel with a partial exhaust return for smoke delivery.

The smoke injection rake was revised to incorporate approximately 12 outlets, each 1 mm in diameter. Testing focused on the relationship between smoke production, delivery resistance, leakage, and dilution by the main airflow.

CAD and Additive Manufacturing

I used Fusion 360 to model the tunnel sections, patterned honeycomb, smoke chamber, and connecting tubes. The CAD/CAM workflow consisted of modelling, slicing, print preparation, and FDM fabrication. Nominal wall thickness was reduced from 5 mm to 3 mm to reduce material consumption while maintaining a practical structure.

The modular construction allowed individual components, smoke routing, and mechanical interfaces to be revised following experimental testing. Dimensional fit, sealing, material consumption, and the thermal limitations of PLA were considered alongside the aerodynamic geometry.

The smoke chamber was subsequently modified to incorporate a food-container screw-on cap for improved sealing. Replacing incense cones with incense sticks and increasing the chamber roof height reduced thermal exposure to the surrounding PLA structure, resolving the warping observed during earlier tests.

03 · Prototype Testing and Design Modifications

Smoke Transport and Chamber Airflow

During initial testing, smoke accumulated within the source chamber without consistently reaching the test section. Opening the chamber lid affected both ember intensity and smoke transport, indicating that airflow through the chamber was an important factor in addition to smoke production. I modified the delivery circuit and incorporated a partial fan-exhaust return to assist smoke transport toward the injection rake.

Smoke Injection Consistency

Smoke discharged from the tubing did not consistently produce distinct streaks within the test section. I investigated injection-rake openings, gaps around the injection assembly, filter resistance, and the balance between the main airflow and smoke supply. These modifications produced visible streaks during successful trials, although consistency across repeated tests remains an area for improvement.

Incense Selection and Orientation

Backflow incense cones initially caused smoke to accumulate near the bottom of the chamber and generated excessive localized heating. Experimental testing indicated that vertically positioned incense sticks provided more effective smoke delivery. Replacing the cones with sticks improved the source arrangement and reduced the thermal exposure that had distorted the original chamber.

Chamber Sealing and Thermal Deformation

The original chamber lid did not provide a reliable seal, and localized heating from the incense cones caused deformation of the surrounding PLA. I incorporated a food-container screw-on cap, replaced the incense cones with sticks, and increased the chamber roof height to provide additional clearance above the smoke source. These modifications resolved the leakage and thermal-deformation problems observed during prototype testing.

04 · Results and Limitations

Prototype Performance

  • Fabricated the modular wind tunnel assembly using FDM-printed components developed in Fusion 360.
  • Obtained visible smoke streaks within the test section during successful experimental trials.
  • Resolved smoke-chamber leakage through the incorporation of a food-container screw-on cap.
  • Eliminated the observed thermal deformation by replacing incense cones with sticks and increasing the chamber roof height.
  • Refined the smoke-delivery circuit, source arrangement, and printed components based on experimental observations.

Future Development

  • Improve smoke-delivery consistency through controlled incense positioning and supply.
  • Conduct repeated visualization trials under consistent fan and incense conditions to assess experimental repeatability.
  • Investigate the relationship between smoke visibility, injection flow rate, and main-tunnel airflow.

The current results are qualitative. Airspeed, flow uniformity, and turbulence intensity have not been quantified for this prototype.
