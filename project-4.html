Project at a glance

The problem

Build an accessible CFD prototype that connects user-drawn geometry with simulated flow and aerodynamic force histories.

What I Developed

  • Implemented a 2D LBM prototype with interactive geometry drawing and autofill.
  • Refined boundary conditions and added force extraction.
  • Compared NACA 0012 shedding frequency with a published numerical benchmark.
Key result

Approximately 1.8% difference in Strouhal number: 1.15 from the solver versus 1.1706 in the reference study.

NACA 0012 · Re = 2,000 · angle of attack = 8°. Benchmark scope: shedding frequency.

Project overview

I am developing an interactive two-dimensional CFD tool using the lattice Boltzmann method (LBM), with the goal of making introductory flow simulation more accessible to beginners, hobbyists, and design teams.

The working prototype allows users to draw a geometry on a grid and simulate flow around it. Implemented features include geometry autofill using breadth-first search, revised inlet and outlet boundary conditions, force extraction, and flow-field visualization. Development has also included collision-operator refinement and a subgrid-model implementation.

The prototype has been exercised with circular and square obstacles and NACA airfoils. A NACA 0012 benchmark compares the frequency of lift oscillations with a published numerical result. The current priority is extending quantitative checks of the solver; a simplified web/cloud interface remains a development goal.

For the benchmark shown here, St = f c / U∞, where f is the shedding frequency, c is chord length, and U∞ is freestream velocity. The solver gives St = 1.15 at Re = 2,000 and an 8° angle of attack. Kurtulus reports St = 1.1706 for this geometry and operating condition using ANSYS Fluent, giving approximately 1.8% relative difference based on the displayed values.

Reference: D. F. Kurtulus, Vortex flow aerodynamics behind a symmetric airfoil at low angles of attack and Reynolds numbers, section on Strouhal number and Figure 20. The comparison supports the shedding-frequency result for this case; further grid, domain, and force-coefficient checks are needed to establish broader accuracy.

Challenges + Solutions

Numerical stability

Maintaining stable simulations across different geometries required refinement of the collision operator and boundary conditions. I iterated on these components and tested the prototype with circular, square, and airfoil geometries.

Quantitative benchmarking

Flow visualizations alone could not establish numerical accuracy. I implemented force extraction and used the periodic lift history to estimate shedding frequency, obtaining a Strouhal number approximately 1.8% from the published reference for the NACA 0012 test case.
