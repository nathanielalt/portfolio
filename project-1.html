Project 01 · UTIAS Research

Aeroacoustics Parametric Research Project

Overview

As part of a research internship at the University of Toronto Institute for Aerospace Studies (UTIAS), I worked under Professor Lavoie in the Fluid Control and Experimental Turbulence (FCET) Laboratory. I was selected as one of two first-year students conducting research at UTIAS and independently led a parametric study of remote microphone probe (RMP) calibration for aeroacoustic wind-tunnel measurements. RMPs provide a convenient alternative to wall-mounted microphones by connecting a remotely located microphone to a surface pressure inlet through a tube. However, propagation through the tube attenuates the pressure signal and introduces a phase shift, requiring the probe to be calibrated using a transfer function (TF). These transfer functions are sensitive to geometric and environmental conditions, which can introduce considerable variability between calibrations and affect experimental results. My study investigated 16 variables, including tube length, tube diameter, temperature, and humidity, to better understand their influence and identify configurations that could improve calibration consistency. I was responsible for the full experimental workflow, from apparatus and test-matrix design through manufacturing, data acquisition, signal processing, statistical analysis, and presentation of the results at the University of Toronto Undergraduate Engineering Research Day (UnERD). I developed a MATLAB acquisition and analysis pipeline that used FFT-based processing to calculate and compare transfer functions across test conditions. Interpreting the results required relating features in the transfer functions, including attenuation, phase shifts, resonant peaks, and other spectral artifacts, to the underlying acoustic and fluid behaviour within the pneumatic tubing. I compared these observations with analytical models of wave propagation, tube resonance, acoustic impedance, and viscothermal attenuation. This included examining how near-wall boundary layers and other tube-flow effects contributed to frequency-dependent pressure losses. To support the study, I designed and manufactured parts using SLA printing. Furthermore, I revised the calibration apparatus used by the group and developed a sensor circuit incorporating I2C and SPI communication protocols. Both the apparatus and sensor circuit were retained by the FCET research group for use in subsequent calibration work. The study also identified several geometric and procedural improvements that I proposed to the group, helping inform its continued RMP research and future aeroacoustic measurement practices. Together, the experimental findings, revised apparatus, sensor circuit, and analysis pipeline provided the group with a more repeatable foundation for future probe calibration and testing.
