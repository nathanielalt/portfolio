Project overview

During a summer research internship at the University of Toronto Institute for Aerospace Studies (UTIAS), I independently led a parametric study of remote microphone probe (RMP) calibration in Professor Philippe Lavoie’s Flow Control and Experimental Turbulence (FCET) Laboratory. I was one of two first-year students conducting research at UTIAS.

RMPs provide a convenient alternative to wall-mounted microphones by connecting a remotely located microphone to a surface pressure inlet through a tube. However, propagation through the tube attenuates the pressure signal and introduces a phase shift, requiring the probe to be calibrated using a transfer function (TF). These transfer functions are sensitive to geometric and environmental conditions, which can introduce considerable variability between calibrations and affect experimental results. My study investigated 16 variables, including tube length, tube diameter, temperature, and humidity, to better understand their influence and identify configurations that could improve calibration consistency.

I was responsible for the full experimental workflow, from apparatus and test-matrix design through manufacturing, data acquisition, signal processing, statistical analysis, and presentation of the results at the University of Toronto Undergraduate Engineering Research Day (UnERD). I developed a MATLAB acquisition and analysis pipeline that used FFT-based processing to calculate and compare transfer functions across test conditions.

Interpreting the results required relating features in the transfer functions, including attenuation, phase shifts, resonant peaks, and other spectral artifacts, to the underlying acoustic and fluid behaviour within the pneumatic tubing. I compared these observations with analytical models of wave propagation, tube resonance, acoustic impedance, and viscothermal attenuation. This included examining how near-wall boundary layers and other tube-flow effects contributed to frequency-dependent pressure losses.

I designed and manufactured SLA-printed components, revised the calibration apparatus, and developed an environmental sensor circuit using I2C and SPI communication. The FCET group retained the apparatus and sensor circuit for subsequent calibration work. The study identified geometric and procedural improvements and provided data and analysis tools for continued RMP research.

Transfer-function smoothness was assessed using curve tortuosity: the ratio of normalized curve length to the straight-line distance between its endpoints. A value of one represents a straight line. Here, tortuosity describes the plotted transfer function, rather than the physical path of the tube. Repeatability was assessed separately using trial-to-trial standard deviation. See the full report for the methods and comparisons.

Results

  • Effectively outlined the relationship between various parameters and RMP transfer functions, significantly increasing the group’s understanding
  • Extracted and experimentally validated RMP improvements informed by insights revealed by the dataset
  • Revised the calibration apparatus, reducing the frequency-averaged, trial-to-trial standard deviation of TF magnitude by 69% and TF phase by 33% relative to the control. A change in speaker orientation also improved coherence below 300 Hz.
  • Delivered valuable data insight and tools (such as the sensor circuit, redesigned calibration apparatus) to the team at FCET to further optimize future RMP configurations.
  • Presented findings at U of T’s UnERD research conference

Challenges + Solutions

  • Sensor Calibration: Ensuring that the sensor housed inside the container accurately reflected the ambient conditions of the room rather than its internal microclimate
  • Experimental Design: Isolating over 16 variables to ensure accurate data between manipulations
    • Isolating and converging on the source of variability
    • Identifying patterns in experimental data and relating them to published models
    • Determining methods to mathematically quantify improvements in the TF
  • Manufacturing: Designing and slicing prints resistant to SLA warping and support failure
